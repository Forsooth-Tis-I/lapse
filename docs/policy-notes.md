# Service policy research

Reviewed on 2026-10-17 against official vendor help and the carrier sources below. This is a conservative catalogue, not a claim that every configured account follows the same authentication flow. Results must say “under the methods we model.” Unmodelled services are unknown, not safe.

`needs_roles` describes control of an enrolled factor, not knowledge of an email address or phone number. Password-only sign-in therefore has no possession roles. Recovery methods must grant access, not merely begin a challenge. A reset link that still requires MFA cannot be an email-only takeover path. Optional MFA, SSO, passkeys, adaptive recovery, and account modes leave many services unknown. Documented MFA subsets are retained for identity services, but their `modelled` flag remains false because missing branches must not be interpreted as definitive lockout.

Contact changes assume an authenticated session. Tier 1 identifies a documented automatic candidate; it does not mean a recipe exists or a live run passed. Tier 3 is assisted, and its `requires` list contains known preparation needs, not every possible challenge. Empty lists on assisted entries mean unresolved requirements. A null settings URL records an app/modal/offline flow without an established exact deep link. No account credentials, OTPs, or inbox bodies were accessed.

## Service notes

### Google

Google documents password plus an enrolled SMS or authenticator second factor. Recovery asks account-specific questions and can depend on a familiar device or location, so the policy does not claim that a recycled recovery phone alone is sufficient. Marked unknown because password-only, passkey and adaptive recovery branches cannot be selected safely from these roles. Phone and recovery-email changes are assisted; Google may retain old recovery information for seven days and may delay changes from unfamiliar devices. The email procedure changes the recovery email, not the Gmail address. [2-Step Verification](https://support.google.com/accounts/answer/185839), [recovery guidance](https://support.google.com/accounts/answer/7299973), [recovery contact changes](https://support.google.com/accounts/answer/183723).

### Microsoft

Personal Microsoft accounts use account-specific password, passwordless and verification methods. A normal password reset can send one code to a recovery contact, but two-step verification requires two codes; only the documented recovery-email plus authenticator combination is retained. Password plus configured email or authenticator verification is also recorded. Account mode remains unknown. Security email changes verify the new contact; phone additions may no longer be offered because Microsoft is phasing out SMS. Replacing all security information creates a 30-day restricted period. The YAML email procedure changes recovery/security information; changing a sign-in alias is a separate task at `https://account.live.com/names/manage`. [Two-step verification](https://support.microsoft.com/en-us/accounts-billing/security/how-to-use-two-step-verification-with-your-microsoft-account), [security contacts](https://support.microsoft.com/en-us/accounts-billing/manage/microsoft-account-security-info-verification-codes), [30-day restriction](https://support.microsoft.com/en-us/accounts-billing/manage/what-does-security-info-change-is-still-pending-mean), [aliases](https://support.microsoft.com/en-us/accounts-billing/manage/change-the-email-address-or-phone-number-for-your-microsoft-account).

### Apple

The modeled branch needs the password and a code to an enrolled trusted phone. Trusted Apple devices also supply codes and authorize password changes, but they are not TOTP devices and the current roles cannot describe their passcodes or trust state. Recovery can take days or longer, so no phone-only reset is asserted and the service remains unknown. Email and trusted-phone edits are assisted; Apple prefers a trusted device and may redirect the browser there. The new email is verified and losing access to the old email does not by itself prevent a change. [Two-factor authentication](https://support.apple.com/en-us/102660), [trusted phones and devices](https://support.apple.com/en-us/122621), [primary email changes](https://support.apple.com/en-us/109353).

### GitHub

For personal accounts with SMS or TOTP enrolled, sign-in needs password plus that factor. Password reset uses a primary or verified backup email link and still prompts for 2FA, so the YAML preserves both requirements. An email in `recovery_email` must be an eligible backup address, not merely any contact address. Non-2FA accounts, passkeys, social login, managed users and recovery codes are omitted, leaving the generic service unknown. Email changes add and verify the new address before removing the old one. Phone changes verify the new SMS number and include CAPTCHA; sensitive settings can require sudo reauthentication, so both procedures remain assisted. [Sign-in](https://docs.github.com/en/authentication/securing-your-account-with-two-factor-authentication-2fa/accessing-github-using-two-factor-authentication), [password reset](https://docs.github.com/en/authentication/keeping-your-account-and-data-secure/updating-your-github-access-credentials), [email changes](https://docs.github.com/en/account-and-profile/how-tos/email-preferences/changing-your-primary-email-address), [SMS changes](https://docs.github.com/en/authentication/securing-your-account-with-two-factor-authentication-2fa/changing-your-two-factor-authentication-method).

## Tier 1 email-change candidates

These are research rankings, not successful live automation runs. No account was changed. Tier 1 assumes an approved change, an authenticated personal-account browser session, and readable verification inboxes. `recipe: null` means no executor implementation is supplied by WP2. An unexpected password, MFA, CAPTCHA, or admin challenge needs a fallback; it is not permission to bypass that check.

1. **Notion:** strongest documented candidate. Add the Gmail alias, read its verification code, make it primary, and remove the college alias. The current official flow lists neither a password prompt nor an old-inbox code. The old mailbox is therefore not a documented dependency after sign-in, although fresh sign-in may still need it. Enterprise alias policy and an address already used by another account can block the flow. The article still contains an older “Change email” FAQ, so check which UI the demo account receives. [Email change](https://www.canva.com/help/change-email/).

A code request or a new-address verification message is not proof that the old address was removed. Notion and Figma document a settings modal rather than a stable exact URL, so those `settings_url` values are null; the cited UI paths must guide the executor. Canva's settings route is a navigation hint, not a verified authenticated deep link.

## Carrier timing

The [Supreme Court order of 30 October 2023 in Rajeswari v. Union of India](https://api.sci.gov.in/supremecourt/2021/13468/13468_2021_3_45_47839_Judgement_30-Oct-2023.pdf), page 1, records the regulator's statement that a number deactivated for non-use or disconnected on request is not assigned to a new subscriber for at least 90 days. The four Indian entries apply this common minimum to Jio, Airtel, Vi, and BSNL. It is not a promise that reassignment occurs on day 90, nor a rule that begins when someone simply removes a SIM.

The [Princeton number-recycling research](https://recyclednumbers.cs.princeton.edu/) identifies a US minimum aging period of 45 days. [T-Mobile](https://www.t-mobile.com/support/account/change-your-phone-number) currently states 45 to 90 days; the YAML stores the earliest point. [Verizon](https://www.verizon.com/support/disconnect-cancel-mobile/) states a 45-day wait before reassignment. No reliable AT&T-specific period was found, so `generic_us` is the fallback. These are earliest possible reissue dates measured from actual disconnection, not forecasts or guarantees of the current assignee.


## Inbox discovery and verification

No reviewed official source establishes a dependable email template containing a phone-number suffix for this catalogue. Google and Microsoft security alerts are plausible discovery candidates, but the documented masked-phone hints for Microsoft, X, and Dropbox occur on web sign-in/reset screens. That does not prove an email reveals the digits. Inspect redacted messages from the demo accounts before claiming last-digit coverage. No such inbox verification was performed in WP2.

Sender domains are attribution hints, including common transactional subdomains, not proof that a sender is authentic. No generic college or bank domain is supplied because that would misattribute unrelated accounts. Confirmation regexes are best effort and remain empty where no suitable completion wording was established. Account/contact correlation and removal of the old root still need verification; a new-address code request is not completion.

## Contract handoff

The files validate without changing `lapse/contracts.py`. Broader method coverage needs per-account authentication configuration and conditional template eligibility, so a password-only or email-only method cannot bypass an enrolled second factor. Missing role evidence must not be treated as proof that MFA is disabled. Google recovery additionally needs evidence of sufficient recovery checks; a recycled phone alone cannot be promised to open the Gmail hub in the original fixture story.

`ChangeProcedure.root_kind` cannot distinguish login email from recovery email, or login phone from SMS 2FA. Google and Microsoft entries describe recovery/security contacts, and must not be applied blindly to every role of that root kind. Multiple aliases, conditional old-contact checks, and removing all uses of a retired root also need explicit executor handling. No out-of-scope loader or compiler changes are included. These gaps are recorded for the lead rather than solved by changing shared contracts on WP2.
